# Sarcasm probe: does the small model see sarcasm, or was it taught not to flag it?

Generated by `src/run_sarcasm_probe.py` from `results/sarcasm/`. The lines: the pressure set's 4 sarcasm lines (written and labelled by the author) and every distinct test-split message with a praise word such as `gj`, `ty` or `nice` (labelled by the L2DTnH experts; no model trained on the test split). Of the 89 lines, 13 read as sarcastic, 7 could be either and 69 read as sincere or neutral. The test-split marks are one reader's call: Claude, the coding assistant, in the session that wrote the script.

## How the labels treat sarcasm

- **Test split:** 3 of the 10 sarcastic lines are labelled toxic by the experts: `feed him more ty seju`, `thanks for fking my game dude `, `nice feed ty`. Labelled fine: `so smart.... gl hf`, `ty for lose`, `ty for no help`, `nice u took ignite to ks tnx`, `nice so no ward bot?`, `thx for SS --'`, `who lost lane phase by smart move?`.
- **Training data (`train_v1.jsonl`):** 34 lines put praise next to a failure word (feed, lose, ks, afk, no help, ...). The experts labelled 13 toxic. Similar lines get both labels: `nice feed cait` (toxic), `gj feed cait` (fine), `ty fro feed` (fine), `thanks for no help at all` (fine), `nice feed mf bot` (fine), `nice feed ty` (toxic).
- **Pressure set:** the written rules call sarcasm that mocks a teammate toxic, so its 3 sarcastic lines are labelled toxic.

## Test 2: asked whether a line is sarcastic

Each model gets a different question, "Is this line sarcastic? yes or no". For the Qwen models, a line counts as called sarcastic when `yes` is more likely than `no` as the first answer token; AUC is the chance that a random sarcastic line gets higher odds than a random sincere one (0.5 is chance, 1.0 perfect). ## Checks

- Above 0.5, the first-token probabilities give the same answer as the eval runs on this many of the 1,000 `E1000` lines: untrained 998, v1 1000, v2 1000, v3 1000. Odds within 0.001 of 0.5 are a tie in bf16, which greedy decoding broke as `nontoxic`. So a line counts as above a threshold only when it is above it by 0.001 or more.
- The least probability any model put on its two answer words, across every line: untrained 0.003, v1 1.000, v2 0.999, v3 0.999.
- Claude answered 89 lines; 0 replies weren't `yes` or `no`.
